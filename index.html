03 — The Digital Bookshelf

Australian public libraries are evolving. Digital What are library collections? The books and other resources available through a library, including printed books, ebooks, audiobooks and digital materials. Collection holdings represent available resources, not borrowing transactions. have expanded, changing the balance between physical and digital holdings. But how are library services distributed across Australia?

Physical vs Digital Collections

Changes in library holdings from 2020–21 to 2024–25.

Physical holdings include printed books and other physical library materials.

Digital holdings include ebooks and other electronic library resources.

Figures are measured in millions of items.

Key finding: Australia's digital library holdings grew by approximately 91%, increasing from 7.33 million items in 2020–21 to 14.02 million in 2024–25. Physical holdings remained comparatively stable, at around 33 million items.

These figures represent collection holdings, not the number of items borrowed.

Source: ALIA/NSLA, Australian Public Libraries Statistical Report 2024–25, page 11.

04 — The Changing Library

Registered library membership has declined while recorded collection usage per person has increased. Explore how these two measures changed between 2020 and 2025.

Follow the years: The line moves from 2020–21 (bottom-right) towards 2024–25 (top-left). Membership decreases while recorded library collection use per person increases.

Key finding: Registered library membership decreased from 35.2% to 28.8% between 2020–21 and 2024–25. However, collection usage increased from 5.6 to 6.6 uses per person.

Collection usage includes physical borrowing and digital transactions, such as ebooks, databases and streaming. The average is calculated across Australia's total population, not just registered library members.

Note: Membership reporting definitions have changed over time. These trends do not establish causation.

Source: ALIA/NSLA, Australian Public Libraries Statistical Report 2024–25, pages 11 and 19.

08 — Books Through the Years

Australia's favourite books span more than a century of publishing history. This beeswarm chart shows the first publication year of each book in ABC Radio National's 2026 Top 100 Australian Books. Each dot represents one book. Dots are spread vertically to prevent overlap.

How to read: Each dot represents one book, positioned according to its first publication year. Dot colour represents its approximate primary genre. Larger dots with gold outlines identify the National Top 10; smaller dots represent ranks 11–100.

Explore the chart: Click a genre in the legend to highlight its books and fade the other genres. Hold Shift while clicking to select multiple genres. Hover over a dot to see the book's title, author, genre, publication year and national ranking.

Key finding: Australia's favourite books include both older classics and modern publications. The selected works range from For the Term of His Natural Life (1874) to titles published in 2025.

This visualisation shows the publication years of books appearing in the Top 100, not the total number of books published in Australia each year.

Sources: ABC Radio National, Top 100 Australian Books (2026); publication years compiled from the annotated Top 100 catalogue and bibliographic records. First publication dates may differ from dates of later editions.

The Last Page

Australia's Reading Story Continues

Australia's reading culture is both enduring and evolving. From the books that have inspired generations to the growth of digital library collections, Australians continue to connect with stories in different ways.

While reading preferences vary across ages and regions, physical books and digital resources remain important parts of the country's reading landscape.

The final page of one book is only the beginning of another.
